# OP-Protagonist Tension Devices — User's 8-Device Framework

The user-defined 8-device taxonomy for keeping OP protagonists tense, supplied via OOB on 2026-08-22 during the Demon-Emperor-Reincarnated session. **Companion to** `op-protagonist-tension-devices.md` (which uses a 6-device canon-isekai-derived framework). Both lenses describe the same problem from different angles and are meant to be applied together — the existing 6-device reference maps to devices 3, 4, 5 of this 8-device framework (Hide / Relationships / Strongest = Antagonist), while devices 1, 2, 7, 8 are unique to this framework.

## When to Use This Reference

Use this file (not just the 6-device reference) when:

- The user explicitly supplies the 8-device list (verbatim or paraphrased) — most common signal is the user posting "Typical ways isekai stories keep tension when the protagonist is OP" with this numbered taxonomy
- The campaign has named *opponents* (Imperial Spymaster, dragon-god analogue, demon court) rather than just the protagonist's own psychology
- The bible needs *external* tension sources — not just "the protagonist feels guilty"

## The Eight Devices

### 1. Escalate the Scale of the Threat

The protagonist is strong locally, so the story zooms out. Country → continental → world-ending threats. Ancient dragons, demon lords, otherworldly invaders, or rival nations that can field armies or multiple high-level threats at once.

**Bible-section binding:** §6 Factions (named L20+ entities the protagonist cannot solo without cost) + §10 Continuity Hooks (dragon-god analogue, demon court succession).

**Verified working shape (Demon-Emperor-Reincarnated 2026-08-22):** Sword-Saint of Karn (neutral, will destroy family if provoked) + dragon-god analogue in southern wastes + Demon Court succession crisis + Imperial Spymaster's standing order. Each of these is a *named, plausible, scale-above-protagonist* threat.

**Anti-pattern:** vague "the world is dangerous" — every named threat needs a specific capability that exceeds the protagonist's toolkit in at least one dimension.

### 2. Shift Away from Pure Combat

Once the MC can win almost any fight, the real challenges become politics, diplomacy, nation-building, resource management, subordinate management, public perception, economics, logistics. *Tensura* (Slime) is the canonical example.

**Bible-section binding:** §6 Factions (named institutional threats) + §10 Continuity Hooks (Malachai, the Quiet Brother) + AI DM footer (every Act should end with a non-combat option carousel).

**Anti-pattern:** every Act ends with "Fight the boss / Negotiate with the boss / Run away from the boss" — there must be non-combat challenge types in every Act's option set.

### 3. Protect Weaker Allies

The protagonist is nearly invincible, but their friends, citizens, or party members are not. Enemies target the people around them, forcing the MC to play defense, strategize, or restrain their power.

**Bible-section binding:** §4 Assets & Retinue (3-4 NPCs who are explicitly lower-level than the protagonist) + §5 Family (siblings + parents are not L12 gestalt).

**Verified working shape:** Maren L3, Lina L6, 3 younger siblings L2-L7, both parents non-magic. *Each one is something the protagonist cannot afford to lose* — that loss has narrative cost, not just mechanical cost.

**Anti-pattern:** making every companion L12+ too — strips the protection dynamic. At least half the named NPCs must be unable to defend themselves against campaign-level threats.

### 4. Put Hard Limits or Costs on the Power

Even "cheat" abilities usually have restrictions: extreme mana/life-force cost, long cooldowns or limited uses, mental strain / corruption / loss of control, specific conditions, social/political consequences for using openly.

**Bible-section binding:** §3 Class & Subclass (any cheat with "Cost: narrative only" needs at least one mechanical limit too).

**Verified working shape:** Foresight = "+10 Initiative, cannot be surprised" with cost = "narrative guilt." That's narrative-only. To strengthen, add at least one of: a mana/Hit-Die cost, a "L7+ draws attention" rule, a "Veil drops for everyone within 100 yards" rule on the L17 unlock. Pair with Device 5 (counters) for mechanical cost.

### 5. Introduce Counters and Specific Weaknesses

New enemies or systems that specifically neutralize or heavily resist the MC's main strength: anti-magic, conceptual attacks, sealed domains, information warfare, named personal weapons.

**Bible-section binding:** §8.5 Counter-Mechanics subsection (new in v1.7.0). For each of the protagonist's named cheats, name 1-2 world elements that specifically counter it.

**Verified working shape (Demon-Emperor-Reincarnated 2026-08-22):**
- **Veilwarden's Sight** (named L18+ witnesses who can see through the Veil) — counter to Foresight+Veil
- **The Quiet Field** (sealed-domain magic that suppresses Bonus-Action casting) — counter to Bonus-Action Any Spell
- **The Chamberlain's Knife** (named obsidian weapon forged to disrupt magical perception) — counter to Permanent Foresight

**Anti-pattern:** do NOT make the counters the same NPC repeated three ways. Each counter should be a different *kind* of threat — passive observer, environmental rule, personal weapon.

### 6. Psychological and Emotional Stakes

The body is strong; the mind is not. Trauma from the first life, fear of losing the new life, guilt, loneliness, identity issues, moral weight of having overwhelming power. *Re:Zero* is the extreme version.

**Bible-section binding:** §2 Personality (3 inner-monologue seeds) + §4 Assets (private death ledger or similar) + AI DM footer (the campaign's question is "what kind of person do they choose to be").

**Anti-pattern:** psychological stakes that are purely mechanical ("low Wisdom = sad") — the bible must name the specific memories, regrets, or guilt sources the protagonist carries.

### 7. Information and Mystery Gaps

The MC is powerful but doesn't fully understand the world's deeper rules, history, or hidden players. Being strong doesn't help if you don't know who the real enemy is or how the system actually works.

**Bible-section binding:** §7 World Lore (with explicit "do not invent" gaps) + §10 Continuity Hooks (each hook names a *specific unknown* the player can choose to investigate).

**Verified working shape (Demon-Emperor-Reincarnated):** Hook 1 = the Reliquary Vault coffer (unknown artifact, unknown owner, unknown purpose); Hook 4 = Malachai's confession (grandmother's deathbed story, unknown contents); Hook 5 = the 3 Veilwardens' Watch (unknown what they're waiting for). Each gap is *bounded* — the player knows what they don't know.

**Anti-pattern:** mystery for mystery's sake — "there's a secret" with no clue trail. Each information gap needs at least one breadcrumb in §7 or §10 that the player can follow.

### 8. Time Pressure and Irreversible Consequences

Even an OP character can't be everywhere at once. Events move forward, people die, political windows close, some outcomes can't be undone no matter how strong you are.

**Bible-section binding:** §10 Continuity Hooks with **fixed in-world day-counts** that close named NPC windows.

**Verified working shape (Demon-Emperor-Reincarnated):** a 90-day Demon Court succession summit that pulls the Quiet Brother out of the Academy on **Day 87**, with the summit itself happening on Day 90 regardless of player action. The "Day-87" sub-deadline is what makes this work — it gives the player a specific NPC-loss event they can choose to prevent, intercept, or accept. Generic ("the summit happens eventually") does NOT satisfy Device 8.

**Anti-pattern:** vague time pressure ("soon") — the bible must include the literal day-count AND a specific named consequence at that day-count.

## Coverage Audit Checklist (Pre-Publish)

Before claiming an OP-protagonist bible is complete, verify all 8 devices are addressed:

- [ ] §6 Factions contains named L12+ threats that exceed the protagonist's toolkit in at least one dimension (Device 1)
- [ ] Every Act's option carousel includes a non-combat challenge type (Device 2)
- [ ] At least 50% of named NPCs are unable to defend themselves against campaign-level threats (Device 3)
- [ ] At least one cheat has a mechanical (not just narrative) cost (Device 4)
- [ ] §8.5 Counter-Mechanics names 2-3 world elements that counter specific cheats (Device 5)
- [ ] §2 Personality + §4 Assets name specific memories, regrets, or guilt sources (Device 6)
- [ ] §7 + §10 contain bounded information gaps with at least one breadcrumb each (Device 7)
- [ ] §10 contains at least one fixed-day-count hook with a named NPC consequence (Device 8)

**Anti-pattern:** shipping a bible that only addresses devices 4 and 6 (the most common failure mode — the protagonist is sad and their power costs them guilt, but nothing else challenges them).
